The three-pillar matrix

Three structures. One building.

Almost every plan we design is some combination of these. Select one to open it.

Term Insulation

Cover the window where a loss would be structural.

Term buys the largest death benefit per dollar, for a fixed span — ten to thirty years. It is sized to the obligations that end: the mortgage, the years until the kids are out, the runway your household needs if your income stops. No cash value, and that is the point; nothing is spent on accumulation.

Most quality term includes a conversion privilege — you can move to permanent coverage without a new medical exam, locked at your original health class, until a deadline. If your health has changed, that clause is often worth more than the policy.

Strategic IUL Accumulation

Growth when the index runs. A floor when it does not.

An indexed universal life policy credits interest tied to a market index, with a floor that stops the credited rate going negative in a down year and a cap that limits it in an up one. Funded correctly and kept under IRS MEC limits, the cash value can be accessed through policy loans without a tax bill, and the death benefit passes income-tax-free.

Downside protection

The floor is not free: policy charges continue in a down year, and gains in an up year are limited by the carrier's cap and participation rate. It is a long commitment and a poor fit for money you may need back early.

Built-In Living Benefits

The policy pays while you are still here to use it.

Living benefits are riders on qualifying policies that release part of your own death benefit early. Critical illness covers a qualifying diagnosis — heart attack, stroke, invasive cancer. Chronic illness triggers when you can no longer perform the activities of daily living unaided. Terminal accelerates the payout on a terminal diagnosis.
