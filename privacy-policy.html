This notice covers Herron & Co. Legacy Agency ("we," "us," "our") and describes how we access, collect, store, use, and share your personal information when you visit our website, request information about life insurance or annuity products, talk to the Legacy Concierge, or otherwise contact us. Your name, phone, email, and mailing address when you ask us for information. If you apply for coverage, the carrier's application also collects underwriting information.

To answer you, quote you, service your policy, market to you if you've opted in, keep our systems secure, and follow the law.

What information we collect

In short

We collect what you hand us, plus basic technical data your browser sends automatically, plus limited data from public and marketing sources.

Information you give us

We collect personal information you voluntarily provide when you register on our services, ask about our products, take part in activities on our site, or otherwise contact us. That typically includes:

  • Names
  • Phone numbers
  • Email addresses
  • Mailing addresses

Everything you give us must be true, complete, and accurate, and you need to tell us when it changes.

Sensitive and underwriting information

We do not collect sensitive personal information through this website, our marketing forms, or the Legacy Concierge. If you move forward with an application, the insurance carrier's application process collects the information required to underwrite the policy — which can include date of birth, Social Security number, income, medical history, prescription history, lifestyle information, driving record, and consumer report data. That information is collected and used only to place and service coverage, and only with your authorization on the application. See our Pennsylvania insurance notice below for how insurance-specific information is handled.

Payment information

If you make a purchase, payment data — including your payment instrument number and its security code — is handled and stored by Ethos. Their privacy notice: privacy.ethoslife.com/policies.

Information collected automatically

Some information is collected automatically when you visit our services. It doesn't reveal your identity on its own, and we use it to keep the site running, secure, and measurable.

  • Log and usage data. IP address, device information, browser type and settings, date/time stamps, pages and files viewed, searches, features used, and error reports.
  • Device data. Information about the computer, phone, or tablet you use, including IP address, device and application identifiers, browser type, hardware model, ISP or mobile carrier, operating system, and configuration.
  • Location data. Precise or imprecise location, depending on your device settings. You can turn this off in your device's location settings, though some features may stop working.

Information from other sources

To improve our marketing and keep our records current, we may obtain information about you from public databases, joint marketing partners, affiliate programs, data providers, social media platforms, and other third parties. This can include mailing addresses, job titles, email addresses, phone numbers, intent data, IP addresses, social media profiles and URLs, and custom profiles, used for targeted advertising and event promotion.

If you interact with us on a social platform using your social account, we receive information from that platform depending on your settings there. Their use of your information isn't governed by this notice.

Google APIs

Our use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

11

Do-not-track signals

In short

There's no agreed standard for DNT signals yet, so we don't respond to them.

Most browsers and some mobile operating systems and apps include a Do-Not-Track ("DNT") feature you can turn on to signal that you don't want your online browsing activity monitored and collected. No uniform technology standard for recognizing and implementing DNT signals has been finalized, so we do not currently respond to DNT browser signals or any other mechanism that automatically communicates your choice not to be tracked online. California law requires us to tell you this. If a standard is adopted that we must follow, we'll say so in a revised version of this notice.

Pennsylvania insurance notice

In short

Pennsylvania has its own rules for insurance information. Here's how we follow them.

In accordance with the Pennsylvania Insurance Data Security Act and 31 Pa. Code Chapter 146a, Herron & Co. Legacy Agency is dedicated to protecting your non-public personal information.

Information we collect

We collect "non-public personal financial information," which can include:

  • Direct information. Name, address, income, and Social Security number provided on applications.
  • Underwriting data. Medical history, lifestyle data, and credit information from consumer reporting agencies.
  • Usage data. IP addresses and browsing activity on our Pennsylvania-facing site.

Disclosure

We do not disclose any non-public personal information about our customers or former customers to anyone, except as permitted or required by law. That includes sharing with affiliates, to provide common insurance and financial services, and with non-affiliated third parties, only as necessary to process or service your transactions — for example, medical examiners or reinsurers.

Data security standards

We maintain physical, electronic, and procedural safeguards that comply with the Pennsylvania Insurance Data Security Act. Our systems are monitored to prevent unauthorized access to your sensitive health and financial records.

Access and correction

Under Pennsylvania law you have the right to access and request correction of the personal information we have on file. Requests must be sent in writing to cherron.insurance@gmail.com.

Fraud notice

As required by Pennsylvania law, we must advise you that submitting false information on an insurance application is a fraudulent act, which is a crime that can lead to penalties and legal action.

Statutory fraud warning

"Any person who knowingly and with intent to defraud any insurance company or other person files an application for insurance or statement of claim containing any materially false information or conceals for the purpose of misleading, information concerning any fact material thereto commits a fraudulent insurance act, which is a crime and subjects such person to criminal and civil penalties."
